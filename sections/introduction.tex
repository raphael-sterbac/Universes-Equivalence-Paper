\section{Introduction}

In type theory, universes allow manipulating types as terms.
To avoid Girard's paradox, Martin-Löf \cite{Martin-Lof-1972, MARTINLOF75} introduced a \textit{hierarchy} of universes: $\U_0, \U_1, \U_2, ...$.
Those can be assumed to be \textit{cumulative}, requiring proper inclusions between them.
Martin-Löf’s first presentations \cite{MARTINLOF75, MARTINLOF82} were formulated à la Russell, where a universe is seen as a type of types. Later, an alternative version à la Tarski \cite{MARTINLOF84}, where a universe is seen as a type of codes along with a decoding function, was also introduced, and Russell universes can be regarded as an informal notation. 

 When presenting Type Theory in an algebraic fashion, Tarski universes are essential if we do not want to introduce sort equations. Indeed, identifying the sort of terms of a universe with a sort of types forces strict set-theoretic equalities that fail in many semantic models. Connected this issue, Voevodsky proposed the Initiality Conjecture project, which aimed at developing rigorous metatheory for a large class of theories. A first step at solving this goal was achieved by Streicher [29], proving a result which can be interpreted as initiality for a fully annotated Calculus of Constructions, and then proving its equivalence with the unannotated formulation. Generalised Algebraic Theories (GATs) provide a framework to describe type theories and their models in a uniform way. In this paper, we extend Streicher’s equivalence results to relate the Generalised Algebraic and Pure Type System presentations. This provides a rigorous justification of the common practice of using informal unannotated syntax with Russell universes, and legitimates interpreting such syntax in the usual models of type theory.

\paragraph*{Related work}
Assaf \cite{assaf} showed the equivalence between Russell and Tarski presentations of universes for the Calculus of Constructions with cumulativity and $\beta$ as an external reduction. A proof extending this result to cumulativity with product covariance was recently published by Felicissimo and Winterhalter \cite{tiago-fscd}, using a technique of \citet{Streicher}. Unfortunately, because these approaches rely on confluence and view $\beta$ as an external reduction, their arguments do not apply to formulations of type theory with judgemental equality, and do not extend to $\eta$-equivalence. This justifies the study of a new proof technique, which applies directly to equational presentations of type theory with $\beta\eta$-conversion.

In the present paper, we will study the simplest form of cumulativity, differing from \citet{tiago-fscd} who retain product covariance. Indeed, structural sub-typing breaks canonicity when adding inductive types \cite{Luo}, and product contravariance does not hold in the set model. Also, it is essential to note that both product covariance and contravariance can be simulated with eta expansion in our setting. Yet, stronger forms of structural sub-typing would provide a more convenient manipulation of universes in practical systems \cite{subtyping}. The recent work of \citet{AdapTT} presents a general framework which would provide automatically the necessary equations to solve the issues mentioned above. In any case, we believe that our work is orthogonal to such considerations, and that our proof should extend to any system that handles structural sub-typing in a correct way.

\paragraph*{Contributions} In this paper we relate the Generalised Algebraic presentation of type theory to the implicit presentation inherited from Pure Type Systems, by a method due to \citet{Streicher}. We considered Martin-Löf's Type Theory with dependent products, cumulative universes and judgmental $\beta\eta$ conversion. Our proof is modular and adapts to various extensions of type theory, such as universe polymorphism \cite{polymorphism}.
Concretely, our equivalence result entails that any semantic model $\mathcal{M}$ of the GAT interprets derivations of the implicit, unannotated Russell theory.
This bridges the gap between the correctness and semantics of explicit presentations, and the implicit theories actually implemented in proof systems.
Additionally, this relates modern semantic results to the literature where implicit syntax was the norm.

The main argument is as follows: there are forgetful maps from the implicit theories to the
implicit presentation, and we show that any implicit judgement can be lifted back in a unique way.
To prove this, we rely on well-known properties of the syntax : no-confusion and injectivity of type formers, which are sometimes referred to as \textit{definitional inversion} \cite{definitional-inv}.
We prove them for the generalised algebraic theory, and they will propagate along our equivalence results, differing from \citet{tiago-fscd} who need injectivity for each theories.
Those properties seem to be strongly related to conversion-checking \cite{meven}, and our equivalence result itself is to be related to the elaboration in proof assistants.

Our proof is elementary and provides an algorithm to translate from one presentation to the other. The definitional inversion properties are proven using the new domain-theoretic technique of \citet{definitional-inv}, making our proof adaptable to various theories which do not enjoy normalisation.
In fact, the proof is purely syntactic and can be done internal to a weak metatheory such as primitive recursive arithmetic, whereas normalisation relies on a stronger metatheory such as the constructive set theory of \citet{Coq19, aczel}.

We first carried out a human-written formalisation of the equivalence between Tarski and Russell universes in Rocq \cite{Formalisation}, assuming the definitional inversion properties. 
In addition, we produced a complete auto-formalisation of the paper in Agda using Claude \cite{auto-formalisation}, as well as an auto-formalisation of the interpretation section (\zcref{sec:semantics}) in Unit \cite{Coquand2026Unit}, allowing us to instantiate our results on concrete models.
This was useful to ensure correctness: we found a subtle mistake in the equivalence proof of \zcref{sectionT2T2'}, and we were able to check that our result extends to universe polymorphism (\zcref{extension}).
The authors carefully audited the formalisation to verify that it faithfully matches the paper and that no postulates were used. Throughout the paper, links to the developments are indicated by the logos \includegraphics[height=.9em]{icon-rocq-blue-eps-converted-to.pdf} and \agdalogo.

\paragraph*{Structure of this paper}
In \zcref{GAT} we present the fully explicit Generalised Algebraic Theory and prove definitional inversion, before presenting the zoo of implicit presentations in \zcref{implicit-presentations}.
Then, we prove that all those syntaxes are equivalent in the following subsections: 
\vspace{0.5em}
\begin{itemize}
    \item[] \textbf{\S\S~\ref{sectionT0T1}.} We relate explicit and implicit substitutions. 
    \item[] \textbf{\S\S~\ref{sectionT1T2}.} We relate Tarski and Russell presentations for cumulative universes.
    \item[] \textbf{\S\S~\ref{sectionT2T2'}.} We relate annotated and unannotated syntaxes. 
\end{itemize}
\vspace{0.5em}
Finally, we extend initiality and give semantics to implicit syntax in \zcref{sec:semantics}, before extending our results to universe polymorphism in \zcref{extension}.
